## A. Measures

All items are seven-point Likert (1 = strongly disagree, 7 = strongly agree) unless noted. Items
are written for a domain-specific administration: `[DOMAIN]` is replaced with the assigned category
(in Study 2, either *clothing and footwear* — high symbolic intensity — or *household energy* — low
symbolic intensity). Reverse-scored items are marked **(R)**.

Items were drafted to satisfy the definitional requirements in `../model/constructs.md` §1.1 and to
be discriminable from the adjacent constructs in §1.2. They have **not** been validated on human
respondents; validation is the first task of the confirmatory programme.

### A.1 Chosen sufficiency (CS) — focal construct

> Anchor: *"Some people set themselves a point at which they have enough of something, and stop
> there. Thinking about [DOMAIN]:"*

1. I have a clear sense of how much [DOMAIN] is *enough* for me.
2. The limit I keep on my [DOMAIN] consumption is one I set myself, not one imposed on me.
3. Staying within my own limit on [DOMAIN] feels like an expression of who I am.
4. I would keep to my limit on [DOMAIN] even if nobody ever knew about it.

*Design notes.* Item 1 carries the bounded-quantity referent; items 2 and 4 carry
self-endorsement (item 4 is the surveillance-independence test that distinguishes integrated from
introjected regulation); item 3 carries positive identity valence. All three definitional features
in §1.1 are represented, which is why a three-item version would be under-identified with respect to
the definition.

### A.2 Route A mediators

**Perceived autonomy (PA)** — limit-specific autonomy need satisfaction

1. The limit I keep on [DOMAIN] is genuinely my own choice.
2. I feel pressured into cutting back on [DOMAIN]. **(R)**
3. I decide for myself how much [DOMAIN] is appropriate for me.
4. When it comes to [DOMAIN], I feel free to consume as much or as little as I judge right.

**Restraint identity affirmation (RIA)** — restraint as self-expansion

1. Keeping within a limit on [DOMAIN] says something good about the kind of person I am.
2. Living with less [DOMAIN] makes me feel more like myself, not less.
3. There is something I value about myself in not needing more [DOMAIN].
4. Cutting back on [DOMAIN] would mean giving up part of who I am. **(R)**

**Restraint signaling value (RSV)** — conspicuous restraint

1. People I care about would think well of me for consuming less [DOMAIN].
2. Being visibly moderate about [DOMAIN] is something worth being known for.
3. I would happily let others see that I keep a limit on [DOMAIN].

**Collective efficacy and norm reinforcement (CE)**

1. If people like me kept limits on [DOMAIN], it would make a real difference.
2. The limit I keep on [DOMAIN] is something I share with others, not something I do alone.
3. People around me are also trying to keep their [DOMAIN] consumption within bounds.

### A.3 Route B mediators

**Anticipated deprivation (AD)**

1. Keeping to a limit on [DOMAIN] would mean going without things that matter to me.
2. My life would be noticeably worse with less [DOMAIN].
3. A limit on [DOMAIN] would stop me doing things I want to do.
4. I could live within a limit on [DOMAIN] without really missing anything. **(R)**

**Psychological reactance (PR)** — adapted from Dillard and Shen (2005); anger and negative
cognition toward the limit-setting agent

1. Being told how much [DOMAIN] to consume irritates me.
2. I resent whoever thinks they can set limits on my [DOMAIN] consumption.
3. Attempts to limit my [DOMAIN] consumption make me want to consume more, not less.
4. I react against being pushed to use less [DOMAIN].

### A.4 Outcomes

**Sufficiency commitment behaviour (SCB)**
1. Over the next six months I intend to keep my [DOMAIN] consumption within a set limit.
2. I am prepared to commit to a specific cap on my [DOMAIN] consumption.
3. I have already taken concrete steps to hold my [DOMAIN] consumption down.

> *Behavioural supplement (recommended for the confirmatory study).* An incentive-compatible
> commitment: respondents choose between a fixed payment and a smaller payment plus a binding
> pledge to a specified reduction, verified at follow-up. This converts SCB from stated to revealed
> and is the single most valuable addition to the measurement battery.

**Brand sufficiency equity (BSE)** — administered after the firm vignette
1. I would trust this company more than others in its category.
2. This company's approach makes me want to support it.
3. I would recommend this company to people I know.
4. This company is doing something genuinely different, not just talking about it.

**Sufficiency policy support (SPS)**
1. I would support policies that cap total consumption of [DOMAIN].
2. Governments should require products in this category to last much longer, even at higher prices.
3. I would support restrictions on advertising that encourages more [DOMAIN] consumption.

**Compensatory displacement consumption (CDC)**
1. If I cut back on [DOMAIN], I would probably treat myself more in other areas.
2. Saving money on [DOMAIN] would free me up to spend more elsewhere.
3. Going without in one area makes me want to make up for it in another.

### A.5 Moderators

> **Note on the simulation.** In Studies 1–3 the five moderators are represented as single observed
> standardised variables, because the object of those studies is the *structural* model and adding
> measurement error to the moderators would confound the power analysis of the structural paths with
> the reliability of the moderators. The batteries below are specified for the confirmatory study,
> where moderators should be measured reflectively and their unreliability propagated. Doing so will
> **attenuate** the interaction terms, so the required sample sizes in Table (Study 1C) should be
> treated as lower bounds — a point that matters most for the two interactions that are already
> the hardest to detect.

**Economic precarity** (M2)
1. I worry about being able to cover an unexpected expense.
2. My financial situation feels stable and predictable. **(R)**
3. I have very little slack in my budget from month to month.
4. Losing my main source of income would put me in serious difficulty quickly.

**Cultural collectivism / interdependent self-construal** (M1) — abbreviated Singelis-style measure
1. What happens to my community matters as much to me as what happens to me.
2. I would sacrifice something I want for the sake of a group I belong to.
3. My sense of who I am depends a lot on the people around me.

**Community embeddedness** (M4)
1. I take part in groups or spaces where people share, repair, or borrow things.
2. I know people I could turn to for help maintaining or repairing what I own.
3. I feel part of a community that values making things last.

**Growth-paradigm endorsement** (M5)
1. A healthy economy has to keep growing.
2. Environmental problems can be solved without reducing how much we consume.
3. Higher consumption is generally a sign of a society doing well.

**Category symbolic intensity** (M3) — manipulated by category assignment in Studies 2–3;
measured for validation
1. What people own in this category says something about who they are.
2. Choices in this category are mostly practical rather than expressive. **(R)**
3. This category is one where people express identity through what they buy.

### A.6 Adjacent constructs (discriminant validity)

**Voluntary simplicity** (Etzioni 1998, adapted)
1. I have deliberately simplified my life overall.
2. I have chosen to live on less than I could afford.
3. Simplicity is a guiding principle in how I live.

**Frugality** (Lastovicka et al. 1999, adapted)
1. I make careful use of what I have rather than replacing it.
2. I take pride in getting good value for money.
3. I resist spending on things I do not strictly need.

**Materialism** (Richins and Dawson 1992, adapted)
1. The things I own say a lot about how well I am doing in life.
2. I would be happier if I could afford to buy more.
3. Acquiring things is an important source of satisfaction for me.

**Constrained restraint** (developed here — the critical contrast case)
1. I consume less [DOMAIN] than I would like to because I cannot afford more.
2. My limits on [DOMAIN] are forced on me by circumstances.
3. If my situation improved, I would consume considerably more [DOMAIN].

> The empirical separation of constrained restraint from chosen sufficiency (HTMT = .120) is the
> measurement result on which the construct's novelty rests. If a confirmatory study finds these
> two constructs collapsing, the theory's central distinction is not measurable and the paper's
> claim fails.

### A.7 Controls recommended for the confirmatory study

Age, gender, education, household income (for precarity discriminant validity), household size,
category expenditure share, political orientation, social desirability (short-form), attention
checks, and a common-method marker variable (Podsakoff et al. 2003).

## B. Experimental stimuli

### B.1 Study 3A — 2 (agency) × 2 (locus) × 2 (frame)

Stringency is held constant at 35% across all eight cells. Only the framing of the *same* 35%
reduction varies. This is what licenses the interpretation of the *d* = .516 best-versus-worst
contrast as a pure design effect.

| Factor | Level | Stimulus wording |
|---|---|---|
| Agency | Chosen | "You will be asked to **set your own** target for reducing your [DOMAIN] consumption. Most people in your position choose around 35%." |
| | Imposed | "A target has been **set for you**: a 35% reduction in your [DOMAIN] consumption." |
| Locus | Collective | "This is a **shared** target. You and others in your area are working toward it together, and progress is reported for the group." |
| | Individual | "This is **your own** target. Progress is reported for you individually." |
| Frame | Sufficiency | "The aim is to find the point where you have **enough** — a level of [DOMAIN] that works well for you and stays there." |
| | Reduction | "The aim is to **cut back** — to consume 35% **less** [DOMAIN] than you do now." |

Manipulation checks: perceived choice over the target; perceived shared-versus-individual framing;
perceived gain-versus-loss framing; perceived magnitude of the reduction (should **not** differ
across cells — this is the critical null check).

### B.2 Study 3B — continuous dose

Seven levels of stringency (5%, 15%, 30%, 45%, 60%, 75%, 90%) with agency set to *chosen* and frame
set to *sufficiency* throughout, so the dose effect is not confounded with architecture. Because the
predicted function is non-monotonic, at least seven levels are required: a three-level design cannot
distinguish an inverted-U from a plateau, and a five-level design locates the turning point with
intervals too wide to test the precarity shift.

### B.3 Study 3C — 2 (demarketing intensity) × 2 (sacrifice signal)

Firm vignettes describing a company in the assigned category.

| Factor | Level | Stimulus content |
|---|---|---|
| Intensity | High | The company actively urges customers to buy less: campaigns advising against replacement, tools to assess whether a purchase is needed, refusal to run seasonal promotions. |
| | Low | The company markets conventionally, with sustainability mentioned among its product attributes. |
| Sacrifice | High | The company discloses that this has **reduced its revenue by 18% over three years**, has closed stores, and has told investors it expects lower volumes permanently. |
| | Low | The company states that the approach is "good for business" and reports **record revenue**. |

The high-intensity/low-sacrifice cell is the operationalisation of **sufficiency washing** and is
the paper's practical target. The low-intensity/high-sacrifice cell — sacrifice claimed without
corresponding action — is the worst-performing cell (*M* = −.178) and models a firm whose stated
commitment outruns its behaviour.

## C. Complete specification of the market-systems model

Notation: consumers *i* = 1…*N*; firms *j* = 1…*J*; periods *t* = 1…*T*.
Reference implementation: `../analysis/src/abm_market.py`.

### C.1 Initialisation

Precarity *z_i* ~ 𝒩(μ_z, σ_z). Baseline demand *q*⁰_i ~ lognormal(0, 0.35), floored at 0.15.

Chosen sufficiency is initialised as a **mixture**, not a single mode:

> cs_i,0 ~ 𝒩(0.50, 0.15) with probability 0.15 (the committed minority)
> cs_i,0 ~ 𝒩(0.15, 0.10) otherwise (the mainstream)

The committed minority is the seed on which any demarketing strategy must survive before contagion
can operate. This is why the population's **variance**, not its mean, is the decisive macro
parameter (P19).

Social network *G*: Watts–Strogatz small world with *k* = 8, rewiring *p* = 0.10; **W** is its
row-normalised adjacency matrix.

Micro-model input, computed once per consumer from the *same* dual-route function estimated in
Studies 2–3:

> g_i = CS(S_req, z_i) — the net dual-route valence of the required limit
> dep_i = max(0, −g_i) — the deprivation excess for consumers past their flip point

Firms: demarketers *j* ≤ *n_dem* receive (d_j, r_j) = (d̄, r̄); others (0, 0). Base prices
*p*⁰_j ~ 𝒰(0.96, 1.04). Installed base IB_j = 1.

### C.2 Per-period sequence

**(1) Sacrifice credibility and price.**

> sac_j = d_j (1 − φ r_j),  φ = 0.40
> p_j = p⁰_j (1 + π_prem · sac_j),  π_prem = 0.15

Credible demarketing earns a willingness-to-pay premium, which is simultaneously a cost in the
choice model: the firm trades volume for margin.

**(2) Choice — the sign-flipped segment elasticity.**

> u_ij = β_leg d_j sac_j cs_i − β_disc d_j (1 − (1 − ρ) cs_i) − β_p p_j
> share_ij = softmax_j(κ_L · u_ij)

with β_leg = 1.20, β_disc = 0.85, β_p = 0.60, ρ = 0.60 (residual discouragement), κ_L = 2.50
(logit scale). Dead firms receive u = −∞.

The residual discouragement ρ is load-bearing. Without it the legitimacy premium is unbounded in
cs_i and the model runs away to a demarketing monopoly — an artefact, not a prediction. Its
substantive interpretation is that a demarketing firm still offers less assortment, less novelty,
and lower availability even to fully committed consumers.

**(3) Demand.**

> q_i = q⁰_i (1 − η cs_i)(1 + ζ rebound_i),  η = 0.55, ζ = 0.60
> units_j = Σ_i q_i share_ij (1 − ν d_j) · (1 − δ r_j),  ν = 0.55, δ = 0.30

The two multipliers separate the demarketing act (ν, own-demand suppression) from durability
(δ, lengthened replacement cycles).

**(4) Firm economics.**

> IB_j ← IB_j (1 − dec(1 − ψ r_j)) + units_j,  dec = 0.10, ψ = 0.65
> rev_j = units_j p_j m_u + r_j IB_j f_s m_s (1 + subsidy),  m_u = 0.22, f_s = 0.19, m_s = 0.38
> cost_j = FC + c_r r_j R̄ + defector_cost (1 − d_j) units_j,  FC = 0.40 R̄, c_r = 0.14
> π_j = rev_j − cost_j

where R̄ is baseline per-firm gross margin revenue. The reconfiguration cost c_r r_j R̄ represents
the capability investment required to decouple revenue from units; without it, reconfiguration
would be free money and the model would trivially favour it.

**(5) Exit.** A firm exits after `exit_patience` = 12 *consecutive* periods with
π_j / π_ref < −0.05, where π_ref is the market's mean profit at the end of burn-in. Requiring
persistence prevents firms dying on a single bad period, which would otherwise kill every
demarketer during its investment phase and make the niche and tipping regimes unreachable.

**(6) Supply-side imitation.** Each period, with probability 0.08, a firm compares its profit with a
randomly chosen rival's and, if the rival is more profitable, moves (d_j, r_j) 20% of the way toward
the rival's. Demarketing therefore spreads or dies **endogenously**.

**(7) Contagion and the micro–macro bridge.**

> exposure_i = Σ_j share_ij d_j sac_j
> cs\*_i = clip( disposition_i + γ g_i + λ (W cs)_i , 0, 1 ),  γ = 0.35
> cs_i ← clip( cs_i + κ exposure_i + θ (cs\*_i − cs_i) + norm_i , 0, 1 ),  κ = 0.08, θ = 0.12
> norm_i = norm_signal · 𝟙[g_i > 0]

Three design decisions matter here.

*Contagion enters the attractor, not the level.* Averaging toward neighbours homogenises a
population; it cannot raise its mean. Because (W cs) is endogenous, the population fixed point is
approximately cs ≈ (disposition + γg)/(1 − λ), making λ a genuine **social multiplier** that
diverges as λ → 1. That divergence is the tipping mechanism.

*The attractor is person-specific.* Relaxation toward cs\*_i rather than toward zero preserves
population heterogeneity, including the committed minority, and lets the micro model shift each
consumer's attractor up (affirmation) or down (deprivation) individually.

*Norm signals are gated on g_i > 0.* Broadcasting a sufficiency norm at consumers who experience the
limit as deprivation does not manufacture consent; it manufactures reactance, handled in (8).

**(8) Reactance and rebound.**

> pressure_i = exposure_i + pol_pressure
> rebound_i ← clip( rebound_i + ξ dep_i pressure_i − 0.10 rebound_i , 0, 1 ),  ξ = 0.55

Reactance accumulates only where a limit is deprivating **and** the consumer is subject to
sufficiency pressure, from firms or from institutions. Being told to consume less is what converts
latent deprivation into active reactance and reactance into compensatory displacement. This is the
macro image of the CDC construct in the micro model, and it generates the backlash regime.

### C.3 Policy instruments

Policy intensity ℓ ∈ [0, 1] sets: repair subsidy = 0.50ℓ; defector cost = 0.09ℓ;
norm signal = 0.010ℓ; consumer-facing sufficiency pressure = 0.60ℓ.

### C.4 Outcome measures

The counterfactual is **business as usual**: no demarketing *and* no policy, same seed. Measuring
against a policy-inclusive baseline would conceal policy-induced backlash, which is one of the
regimes the theory predicts.

> Δthroughput = (Q_base − Q_treat) / Q_base
> leakage = 1 − (Q_base − Q_treat) / (units forgone at demarketers)
> relative profit = π̄_demarketers,treat / π̄_all,base
> PMV = 1 iff (no demarketer exit) ∧ (relative profit ≥ 0.95) ∧ (Δthroughput > 0.10)

**Regime classification** (order matters; failure modes are diagnosed first):

| Regime | Condition |
|---|---|
| Backlash | Δthroughput < −0.02 |
| Collapse | demarketer exit, or relative profit ≤ 0 |
| Futility | Δthroughput < 0.05 |
| Niche | 0.05 ≤ Δthroughput < 0.20 and viable |
| Tipping | Δthroughput ≥ 0.20 and viable |
| Strained | reduction achieved only by eroding demarketer profitability |

Only **demarketer** exit counts as collapse. A conventional volume incumbent exiting during a
successful transition is incumbent displacement, not failure; conflating the two mislabels the
theory's success case as its failure case. (This was a genuine bug in an earlier version of the
analysis, caught by inspecting the phase diagram: the high-λ, high-*r*, high-policy corner was being
coded as "collapse" because incumbents were dying.)

## D. Study 4 calibration search

The theory predicts five qualitatively distinct regimes. A model that cannot produce all five is not
a test of the theory; a model that produces any regime for any parameters has no content. We
therefore searched the three firm-economics parameters that govern the value of reconfiguration for
a region in which all five regimes are reachable **from their theoretically specified
configurations**, and report the search rather than asserting the result.

Grid: service fee ∈ {0.115, 0.140, 0.165, 0.190} × reconfiguration cost share ∈ {0.10, 0.14, 0.18}
× repair-subsidy coefficient ∈ {0.35, 0.50, 0.80}. 36 cells, each evaluated on all five scenarios.

Selected parameterisation: **service fee 0.19, reconfiguration cost share 0.14, subsidy coefficient
0.50** — chosen as interior in two of three dimensions rather than for maximum effect size.
All five regimes are reproduced. Full search: `../analysis/output/tables/calibration_search.csv`;
script: `../analysis/src/calibrate_abm.py`.

**Disclosed limitation.** The service fee sits at the upper bound of the searched grid. Values below
approximately 0.165 make the niche regime unreachable (the reconfigured demarketer becomes
"strained" rather than viable), because reconfiguration is then insufficiently remunerative to cover
its own capability cost. The structural results — complementarity, the λ\*(r) frontier, the
inequality result — hold throughout the region where all five regimes exist, but the *level* of the
tipping regime's profitability is sensitive to this parameter and should not be quoted as a forecast.

Similarly, the micro parameters were calibrated so that the limit-architecture levers produce
vignette-experiment effect sizes in the *d* = .20–.40 range with a best-versus-worst contrast near
*d* = .6 (`../analysis/src/calibrate_micro.py`). Study 1's power curves are conditional on those
magnitudes; halving them roughly quadruples the required sample sizes.

### E.2 Known fragilities, stated plainly

1. **The two-lines breakpoint is set at the estimated quadratic turning point** rather than by
   Simonsohn's Robin Hood procedure. Because the estimated turning point is itself biased upward by
   quadratic asymmetry, the descending-arm test is mildly conservative and the ascending-arm test
   mildly liberal. Neither reverses at the observed effect sizes.

2. **The quadratic turning point is an upward-biased estimator of the flip point** when the underlying
   function is concave-saturating minus convex-accelerating: .406 estimated versus .353 analytic. Any
   empirical estimate of a flip point from a polynomial model inherits this bias. A segmented or
   shape-constrained estimator would be preferable and is recommended for the confirmatory study.

3. **The tipping regime's profit multiple (5.7×) is not credible as a forecast.** It is sensitive to
   the service-fee parameter (see §D) and to the assumption that installed-base service revenue
   scales without diminishing returns, which is certainly false in real service businesses.

4. **Leakage is bounded at 1.5 and −3.0 in code.** The backlash scenario reports leakage = 1.500,
   which is the cap: in that scenario the demarketers' own volume loss is small while aggregate
   throughput *rises*, so the ratio is unstable. The reported value should be read as "leakage
   exceeds 1", not as a point estimate.

5. **The micro model has no cross-domain structure.** Compensatory displacement is an outcome, not a
   portfolio process. A consumer's sufficiency bounds across domains almost certainly interact, and
   this is exactly where leakage lives.

6. **Reflective measurement throughout.** Business-model reconfiguration and community embeddedness
   are arguably formative; treating them reflectively in a confirmatory study would be a
   misspecification.

7. **Study 4 contains no politics.** Policy is exogenous. A model of sufficiency policy that treats
   policy as exogenous omits the main obstacle to it.

### E.3 Checks a reviewer will ask for that we have *not* done

- Common-method bias assessment beyond the marker factor in the DGP (needs real data).
- Test–retest reliability and temporal stability of chosen sufficiency as a state construct.
- Incentive-compatible behavioural validation of sufficiency commitment.
- Measurement invariance across *categories* (apparel versus energy), as opposed to across countries.
- Scalar (intercept) invariance, which permits latent-mean comparison across countries. Only
  configural and metric invariance are established here; the paper therefore makes no claims about
  cross-national differences in construct *levels*.
- Sobol or Morris global sensitivity analysis of the ABM; only one-factor-at-a-time is reported,
  which cannot detect interaction effects among parameters.

```bash
# One-time environment setup (Python 3.11)
python -m venv .venv && . .venv/bin/activate
pip install -r analysis/requirements.txt

cd analysis/src

python run_all.py            # full pipeline, ~13 min, deterministic (SEED = 20260801)
python run_all.py --quick    # reduced replications, ~2 min
python run_all.py --skip-mc  # everything except the Monte Carlo study

python calibrate_micro.py    # micro effect-size calibration
python calibrate_abm.py      # Study 4 regime-reachability search
python verify_manuscript.py  # checks all 408 numeric claims in MANUSCRIPT.md

cd ../../paper
python build_documents.py    # builds dist/*.html (figures embedded) and dist/*.docx
```

`verify_manuscript.py` is the important one. It re-reads every results table and asserts that each
numeric claim in the manuscript matches, exiting non-zero on any mismatch. It currently reports
**408/408 claims verified**. It caught eleven genuine errors during drafting, including one
substantive misstatement (a product-term interaction described as non-significant when it is
significant at *p* = .006). Any future change to the model or parameters that invalidates a
manuscript claim will be caught by running it.

### Module map

| File | Role |
|---|---|
| `config.py` | All parameters; the single source of truth for the calibration |
| `dgp.py` | The formal micro theory: route functions, closed-form flip point, latent simulation, measurement model, study datasets |
| `structural.py` | The canonical estimator, reused by Studies 1, 2 and 3 so that the power analysis is a power analysis *of the reported estimator* |
| `measurement.py` | Study 1: CFA, reliability, HTMT, Monte Carlo recovery, power |
| `sem_micro.py` | Study 2: latent SEM, configural fit, invariance, multigroup |
| `moderated_mediation.py` | Study 2: bootstrapped mediation system, indices of moderated mediation, Johnson–Neyman |
| `experiment_analysis.py` | Study 3: factorial ANOVA, two-lines test, flip-point bootstraps |
| `abm_market.py` | Study 4: market-systems model, regimes, tipping surface, inequality, sensitivity |
| `calibrate_micro.py`, `calibrate_abm.py` | Calibration transparency |
| `figures.py` | All 12 figures |
| `run_all.py` | Master pipeline |
| `verify_manuscript.py` | Manuscript-to-output consistency harness |
