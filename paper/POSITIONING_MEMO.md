# Positioning and Reviewer-Risk Memo

**Paper:** *Marketing Without Growth: A Market-Systems Theory of Sufficiency, Demarketing, and
Consumer Sovereignty Under Limits*

Internal document. Not for submission. Purpose: state honestly where this paper is strong, where it
is vulnerable, and what has to be true for it to survive FT50 review.

---

## 1. The one-sentence claim

Whether a consumption limit expands or contracts consumer sovereignty depends on how the limit is
*designed*, and whether a market can contract without collapsing depends on the joint presence of
business-model reconfiguration and norm propagation — so the discipline usually blamed for
overconsumption holds the design tools for its remedy.

If a reviewer cannot restate that after the introduction, the introduction has failed.

## 2. Journal fit

### Journal of Public Policy & Marketing — **lead target**

*Why.* The paper's sharpest and most defensible result is distributive: precarity lowers the micro
flip point, which raises the macro tipping threshold, which becomes unattainable beyond a precarity
level. That is a public-policy finding with a clear sequencing prescription (distributive policy
before sufficiency policy), derived rather than asserted. JPP&M is also the outlet most tolerant of a
theory-plus-simulation paper with a stated pre-registration status, and most interested in the
Johnson–Neyman result that stringency is counterproductive for the majority of the precarity
distribution.

*Fit signals.* JPP&M has an active interest in wiser-consumption and sufficiency themes, including a
recent call in that territory. The paper speaks directly to campaign calibration, claim
substantiation standards for "buy less" messaging, and backlash as a forecastable intervention
effect.

*What to lead with.* The 54.6% figure and the inequality result. Not the ABM.

### Journal of Marketing (Marketing & Society) — **strong second**

*Why.* P21 — marketing capability is regime-determining — is a claim about the discipline's
constitutive relevance, which is the Marketing & Society section's remit. The formal demonstration
that throughput reduction and firm profitability are jointly attainable is a *Journal of
Marketing*-shaped result: counterintuitive, managerially consequential, formally derived.

*Risk.* JM will want field data. The synthetic status of Studies 1–3 is a serious obstacle here in a
way it is not at JPP&M. Realistic path: run the confirmatory programme first, then submit with real
Studies 2–3 and the ABM as Study 4.

*What to lead with.* Study 3C (sufficiency washing) and the futility/tipping contrast.

### Journal of the Academy of Marketing Science — **good fit for the full package**

*Why.* JAMS is the most receptive to multi-method papers combining measurement development,
moderated mediation, experiments, and simulation, and to papers that hand the field a validated
instrument plus a design specification. Sheth, Sethia and Srinivas's mindful-consumption paper
appeared here, which establishes the conversation.

*What to lead with.* The construct, the discriminant validity against four adjacent constructs, and
the power analysis as a design contribution.

### Journal of Consumer Research (conceptual track) — **highest risk, highest reward**

*Why.* The dual-route mechanism with a derived nonlinearity, and the CCT extension into
"consumption under limits", are JCR-shaped theoretical contributions.

*Risk.* JCR will be least sympathetic to the ABM (it will read as out of place) and most demanding
about the phenomenological richness of chosen sufficiency. A JCR version should probably drop Study 4
entirely and add qualitative grounding.

### Where **not** to send it

*Journal of Macromarketing* and *Marketing Theory* would accept it more readily and defeat the
purpose. The stated objective is to move this conversation into FT50-tier outlets; publishing in the
journals that already host it forgoes the first-mover positioning that motivates the paper.

## 3. The five reviewer objections that can kill this paper

### R1. "The data are simulated. This is not an empirical paper."

**Severity: fatal if mishandled; manageable if handled first.**

*The wrong response* is to bury the disclosure, use language like "model-derived data", or imply the
synthetic studies are evidence. Reviewers will find it, and the paper will be desk-rejected for
misrepresentation rather than reviewed for content.

*The response we have adopted.* A boxed disclosure before the abstract, the word "synthetic" in the
studies overview table, and a reframing of Studies 1–3 as a **computational pre-registration** whose
deliverable is a specification rather than a finding. The paper claims exactly three things from
them: the model is identified and estimable; the construct is discriminable; and specific hypotheses
require specific designs. All three are true claims about a model, not about the world.

*The argument that carries it.* The power analysis produced a result that no amount of theorising
would have produced: the demarketing-credibility interaction is **undetectable in a survey at any
realistic N**. A field team that had not run this would have burned a data collection discovering it.
That is a genuine service to a pre-paradigmatic literature, and it is only obtainable by simulation.

*Residual risk.* At JM and JCR this may still be disqualifying. JPP&M is the target partly for this
reason.

### R2. "This is ideologically loaded advocacy, not theory."

**Severity: high. This is the objection the topic attracts.**

*Our defences, in order of strength.*

1. **The model rejects one of our own conjectures.** P22 (credibility–viability tradeoff) is
   specified, tested at four erosion rates, and rejected. Advocacy does not do this.
2. **The theory's own failure mode is inside the model.** Compensatory displacement is a measured
   outcome and reactance-driven rebound generates a regime in which the intervention *increases*
   throughput by 23.6%.
3. **The modal result is negative.** 38% futility and 20% collapse at zero policy. If we were
   advocating, the modal result would be tipping.
4. **The distributive critique of degrowth is derived as correct** under stated conditions, rather
   than argued away. We say explicitly that the "politics of the comfortable" objection is a correct
   description of sufficiency policy without distributive policy.
5. **Boundary conditions are specified, not gestured at.** Five moderators with directional
   predictions, one of which (symbolic intensity) predicts *polarisation* rather than uniform
   dampening.

*What we must not do.* Use normative language in the theory sections. The words "should", "must", and
"urgent" belong only in the implications sections, and even there sparingly. Current draft is
reasonably disciplined; a final pass should hunt for advocacy register.

### R3. "This is not really marketing."

**Severity: moderate. Fully answerable.**

The antecedent is a designed communication object (limit architecture) and a firm strategy
(demarketing intensity, sacrifice signalling, business-model reconfiguration). The mediators are
consumer appraisals. The outcomes include brand equity. The macro layer is a competitive market with
share, margin, entry, exit and imitation. The headline managerial finding concerns when a brand can
position on selling less. §1.2 makes this argument explicitly and early, which is where it belongs.

Reinforcement available if needed: the paper is continuous with an existing marketing literature —
Kotler and Levy (1971) on demarketing, Peattie and Peattie (2009) on consumption reduction,
Gonzalez-Arcos et al. (2021) on resistance to sustainability interventions, White et al. (2019) on
behaviour change, Giesler and Fischer (2017) on market system dynamics, Rand and Rust (2011) on
agent-based rigour. Every methodological move has a marketing precedent.

### R4. "The agent-based model's parameters are arbitrary."

**Severity: moderate.**

*Defences.* The calibration search is reported, with the grid and the selected cell
(`calibration_search.csv`), and the selected cell is interior in two of three dimensions. Sensitivity
analysis covers 16 parameters at ±20%, all 32 perturbations preserving the regime. The claims we make
are explicitly **structural** (which regimes exist, their signatures, which factors are complements,
comparative statics) and we state that point predictions are not supported. We disclose that the
service-fee parameter sits at the grid's upper bound and that values below ~0.165 make the niche
regime unreachable.

*Weakness we cannot fully answer.* Only one-factor-at-a-time sensitivity is reported. A Sobol or
Morris global analysis would detect parameter interactions and should be added before submission. We
list this in the appendix as a check not performed.

### R5. "Chosen sufficiency is voluntary simplicity with a new name."

**Severity: moderate. This is the construct-proliferation objection and it is legitimate.**

*Response.* HTMT = .531 against voluntary simplicity — related, not redundant. More decisively,
HTMT = .120 against **constrained restraint**, which is the same reduction behaviour without
endorsement. The construct is not measuring behaviour; it is measuring a relationship to behaviour,
and voluntary simplicity cannot make that distinction because it is defined as a lifestyle rather
than a state.

The stronger argument is functional. Chosen sufficiency is a **state with designable antecedents**;
voluntary simplicity is a trait. Study 3A moves chosen sufficiency by *d* = .516 through design
alone, at constant stringency. Nothing in the voluntary-simplicity tradition predicts or explains
that, because traits do not respond to framing.

*Must-do.* The confirmatory study must include incremental-predictive-validity tests: chosen
sufficiency predicting behaviour, brand equity, and policy support **over and above** voluntary
simplicity and low materialism. If it does not, the construct is redundant and the paper fails.

## 4. Objections we expect but consider manageable

| Objection | Response |
|---|---|
| "The inverted-U is just a quadratic." | Two-lines test (Simonsohn 2018) with both arms significant; replicated within each precarity half. |
| "The flip-point shift rests on a tercile split." | Primary test is a bootstrap on the turning-point *difference*; the stringency²×precarity product term converges (*p* = .006), and the *linear* interaction is null, which is the mechanism-specific signature. |
| "Metric invariance via permutation is non-standard." | Justified on distributional grounds and standard in composite modelling; we make no latent-mean comparisons, so scalar invariance is not required. |
| "Four countries cannot support claims about the Global South." | We say so explicitly in the limitations and frame the invariance result as a hypothesis. |
| "Leakage of 1.500 looks like a cap." | It is; disclosed in the appendix, with instruction to read it as "exceeds 1". |
| "Why is precarity a moderator rather than a control?" | It enters the deprivation cost function's *curvature*, not its level — a theoretical claim with a distinctive empirical signature that the data pattern supports. |

## 5. What has to be true for the paper to survive

1. **The construct must be discriminable on real data.** Specifically, chosen sufficiency must
   separate from constrained restraint and show incremental validity over voluntary simplicity.
2. **The inverted-U must replicate with human participants**, on the two-lines test, not just the
   quadratic.
3. **The flip point must move with precarity.** This is the paper's distinctive prediction and the
   basis of its policy claim.
4. **Study 3C must replicate.** The sufficiency-washing null is the most managerially consequential
   single result and the easiest to test.
5. **The ABM must survive global sensitivity analysis**, not just one-factor-at-a-time.

If (1)–(3) fail, the theory is wrong and should be abandoned rather than salvaged. That is the
appropriate standard.

## 7. Honest assessment of novelty

The claim in the brief was "breakthrough — genuinely pre-paradigmatic territory". Our assessment
after building the model:

**Genuinely novel.** The dual-route architecture with a derived flip point; the demonstration that
*S\** moves with precarity; the formal treatment of demarketing propagation with a leakage measure;
the strict-complementarity result; the micro-to-macro derivation of the inequality constraint; the
polarisation prediction for symbolic categories; the sufficiency-washing asymmetry.

**Novel in this domain but not in general.** The saturating-versus-accelerating mechanism is a
familiar functional form in economics and psychology; its application here is new, the form is not.
Social-multiplier tipping is well established in the social-norms literature (Centola et al. 2018;
Nyborg et al. 2016); the contribution is the marketing-specific transmission channel and the
firm-viability coupling.

**Not novel, and should not be claimed as such.** That sufficiency must be chosen rather than
imposed; that demarketing risks free-riding; that degrowth raises distributive concerns. These are
all present in the existing literature as arguments. The contribution is converting them into
derived, quantified, falsifiable statements — which is a real contribution, and a different one from
discovering them.

**The honest headline.** This is not a new insight dressed as a model. It is a set of existing
arguments converted into a theory with closed-form implications, specified boundary conditions, a
falsifiable measurement model, and a formal market-level result that the literature has asserted
without deriving. That is a strong contribution. Overclaiming it as a discovery would invite exactly
the ideology objection the paper is otherwise well defended against.
