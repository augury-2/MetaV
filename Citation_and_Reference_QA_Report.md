## 2. The critical finding

**The submitted file contained no reference list.** Section 1.11 promises "the consolidated
references" at the end of the volume, but the document ends at Section 8.6 (Directions for Future
Research). Every one of the 833 in-text citations was therefore an orphan. Building and verifying a
complete APA 7 reference list was consequently the largest single element of this audit.

A second, equally serious finding emerged during verification: **28 cited works could not be
traced to any real publication.** Each was checked against Crossref DOI metadata, publisher
records, institutional repositories and the wider literature. Where a claim was genuine but the
attributed source was not, the citation was replaced with a verified source that supports the same
statement; nothing was invented to fill a gap.

## 5. APA 7 formatting corrections applied

1. **Ampersand rule.** 153 parenthetical citations converted from `and` to `&` (e.g. `Negi and Anand, 2015` → `Negi & Anand, 2015`). Narrative citations correctly retain `and`.
2. **`et al.` from first use.** Seven long author strings shortened (Gardas, Raut & Narkhede; Stern, El-Ansary & Coughlan; Cadilhon, Fearne, Tam, Moustier & Poole; Aramyan, Oude Lansink, van der Vorst & van Kooten; Stević, Pamučar, Puška & Chatterjee; Mulvey, Vanderbei & Zenios; Saunders, Lewis & Thornhill; Murthy, Gajanana, Sudha & Dakshinamoorthy).
3. **Same-year, same-first-author disambiguation.** The two distinct Murthy 2009 studies are now distinguished by additional surnames, as APA 7 requires when `et al.` would be ambiguous.
4. **Lowercase particle.** `Van Laarhoven` → `van Laarhoven`; the truncated narrative form `Laarhoven and Pedrycz (1983)` corrected.
5. **Group authors and abbreviations.** `MoAandFW` → `MoA&FW`; the abbreviation is introduced in full at first use in Section 1.1 and used consistently thereafter; the reference entry is filed under *Ministry of Agriculture and Farmers Welfare*, matching the in-text form.
6. **Software citation.** `Jamovi Project, 2023` → `The jamovi project, 2023`, with a version-and-medium reference entry.
7. **Newspaper titles.** `Times of India` → `The Times of India` (retained only where a press source is genuinely required — see §8).
8. **Missing punctuation.** `Webster and Watson 2002` → `Webster & Watson, 2002`.
9. **Alphabetical ordering** within every multi-source parenthetical group (59 groups re-ordered).
10. **Reference list.** Strict alphabetical order; hanging indent of 0.5 in; double line spacing; sentence-case article and book titles; title-case journal names; journal name and volume italicised; issue number roman and in parentheses; en-dashes in page ranges; DOIs rendered as `https://doi.org/...`; editions, publishers and repository handles supplied.

Chapters 7 and 8 were the weakest points in the scholarly apparatus: the Discussion made repeated
appeals to "a venerable tradition in smallholder marketing studies", "the perishables literature"
and "evidence that the most significant portion of postharvest loss…" without a single supporting
citation, and Chapter 8 carried none at all. These passages are now anchored to the literature they
invoke. Methodological statements in Chapter 4 (sampling frame logic, content validity, fuzzy scale
construction, ELECTRE thresholds, replicability) were similarly supported with seminal sources
(Cochran, Kish, Churchill, Lawshe, Hinkin, Zadeh, Buckley, Chang, Roy, Hatami-Marbini & Tavana).

Citations were deliberately **not** added to paragraphs that report the study's own numerical
results, to table and figure cross-references, or to objective and research-question lists, since
citation there would be inappropriate rather than rigorous.
